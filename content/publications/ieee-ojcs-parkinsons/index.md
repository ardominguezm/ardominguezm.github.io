abstract: >
  Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that affects both motor and speech functions.
  This study proposes a comprehensive mathematical framework for PD classification that integrates topological,
  statistical, and spectral representations of speech signals. The framework combines descriptors derived from
  persistent homology, random matrix theory, and frequency-domain analysis. A hybrid training strategy is employed,
  using synthetic speech data for training and real samples exclusively for evaluation. Experimental results show
  that spectral features, especially when fused with statistical descriptors, achieve the highest discriminative
  power, reaching 98.00% accuracy and 97.98% F1-score with a multi-layer perceptron classifier.

summary: >
  A hybrid mathematical framework combining spectral, statistical, and topological features achieves state-of-the-art
  accuracy in voice-based Parkinson’s disease detection.
